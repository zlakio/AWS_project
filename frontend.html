Plain local search found no measurable improvement over HEFT in this benchmark (makespan and cost are effectively identical) — it gets stuck at a local optimum with no single-mutation improvement available. That is the motivation for the cost-aware search using iterated (perturbation-based) local search rather than one greedy pass.

Dynamic repair: selective vs. naive

A random VM fails partway through each of 20 running schedules. Selective re-optimization repairs only the affected tasks and their downstream dependents; the naive baseline reschedules every unfinished task from scratch with plain HEFT.

Selective repair reaches a resulting makespan within 0.4% of the naive full reschedule — the speed and stability gains are not paid for with worse schedule quality.

Methodology. Gantt example: 14 tasks, 5 DAG levels, 4 VMs, seed 23, α=0.55 β=0.3 γ=0.15. Static benchmark: 20 tasks, 6 DAG levels, 5 VMs, 500 local-search iterations, seeds 1000–1029. Dynamic benchmark: 25 tasks, 7 DAG levels, 6 VMs, VM failure injected at 40% of the original makespan, seeds 2000–2019. All averaged figures are computed by core/benchmark.py in the DCA-HEFT-ILS repository. VM pricing and disruptions are simulated, not measured from a live cloud.
